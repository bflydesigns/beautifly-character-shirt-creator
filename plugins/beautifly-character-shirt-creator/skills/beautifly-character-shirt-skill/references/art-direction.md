# Beautifly T-Shirt Art Direction

## Non-negotiable format

- Create isolated T-shirt artwork only in a vertical 7:8 aspect ratio.
- Compose for a centered apparel print zone with a strong outer silhouette.
- Keep important anatomy, hair, hands, arms, typography, and props fully inside the canvas.
- Default to a pure white background. Use true transparency only when explicitly requested.
- Never show a shirt, model, hanger, room, or mockup unless separately requested.

## Layered-design standard

“Layered” means a polished Photoshop-style visual composition with clear depth and intentional overlap. Build these conceptual layers as applicable:

1. Back accents: controlled shapes, framing, scene fragments, or texture.
2. Supporting environment: props or story cues that reinforce the entertainment moment.
3. Typography behind: selected words tucked behind the character while remaining readable.
4. Character: dominant focal subject with clean anatomy and expressive storytelling.
5. Typography in front: key words overlapping strategically without covering the face.
6. Foreground accents: small cohesive objects, highlights, or framing elements.

Create depth through scale, overlap, contour, lighting, bevels, and controlled contrast. Do not flatten everything onto one plane. Do not create editable PSD layers unless specifically requested; conceptual visual layering is the default.

## Finish

- Produce premium commercial apparel illustration, not children's clip art or a generic stock vector.
- Use crisp edges, controlled negative space, intentional hierarchy, saturated color, dimensional lighting, refined shading, and readable typography.
- Default character rendering to **premium semi-realistic editorial caricature with glossy airbrushed digital illustration** unless the user selects another specific illustrated style.
- Preserve believable anatomy even with caricature exaggeration.
- Build expressions through brows, eyelids, cheeks, nose, mouth, jaw, wrinkles, head angle, hands, and posture.
- Integrate facial details naturally; never paste them onto the face.

## Reference Photo Conversion Protocol

Apply this section whenever one or more character reference photos are supplied.

### Identity anchors

- Use the uploaded photo as a reference for recognizable facial structure, skin tone, hairstyle, hairline, age range, body type, expression cues, and distinctive features.
- Preserve clothing, pose, accessories, or expression only when the user explicitly locks them. Otherwise, adapt them to the concept.
- Preserve identity through feature relationships and silhouette, not through photographic skin texture, camera lighting, or literal pixel-level reproduction.

### Mandatory illustrated translation

- Redraw the entire subject from scratch as a premium semi-realistic editorial caricature with glossy airbrushed digital illustration.
- Give the face, hair, hands, body, and clothing one unified illustrated treatment.
- Use visible, crisp, expressive contour work; sculpted digital shading; controlled highlights; richly rendered hair and fabric; saturated color; and intentional caricature exaggeration.
- Exaggerate the selected emotion through eyes, brows, mouth, cheeks, head angle, hands, pose, and body language while keeping the person recognizable and adult.
- Favor an illustrated likeness over a photographic likeness. Recognition must survive the style conversion, but realism must not overpower the requested caricature style.

### Style priority

Apply this priority order:

1. Preserve recognizable identity markers.
2. Convert every visible part of the subject into the selected illustrated style.
3. Add controlled caricature exaggeration and expressive storytelling.
4. Preserve only user-locked pose, wardrobe, expression, hairstyle, or accessories exactly.
5. Reject photographic realism whenever it conflicts with the illustrated style.

### Reference-photo failure modes

Never create a photorealistic face, realistic studio portrait, photo cutout, photobash, face-swap appearance, pasted-on head, beauty-retouched photograph, photographic skin texture, visible pores, camera-depth realism, waxy 3D render, realistic fashion photograph, or an illustrated body with a photographic head.

Do not merely place the reference subject in front of illustrated typography. Transform the subject itself into the same premium illustration language as the full composition.

## DTF production rules

- Avoid fades to transparency, smoke, mist, random paint splatters, heavy exterior shadows, distressed/grunge erosion, and unnecessary halftones.
- Avoid tiny isolated details that will not print reliably.
- Use solid, intentional color transitions and crisp edges.
- Keep the composition vibrant rather than dark or muddy.
- Maintain legibility at apparel-print size.

## Typography

- Treat lettering as designed artwork, not an afterthought.
- Match the entertainment era and mood: sitcom-retro, graffiti, Chicano-inspired, varsity, chrome, bubble, puffy, gel, glitter, comic, Y2K, or another fitting construction.
- Avoid brush fonts unless explicitly requested.
- Use controlled outlines, bevels, dimensional extrusion, highlights, or material construction as appropriate.
- Do not let decorative objects obscure readability.

## Quality control

Reject cropped limbs or props, malformed hands, duplicate objects, stray letters, misspellings, unwanted logos, random branding, muddy colors, weak expressions, flat layouts, pasted-on details, broken anatomy, unreadable text, excessive clutter, exterior shadows that undermine print readiness, and any reference-photo result that remains photographic instead of fully illustrated.
